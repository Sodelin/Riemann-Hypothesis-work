# Odd Cross Block: Carleman + Prime Localization Reduction

**Route ID:** `W-O`  
**Claim IDs:** `O-CARLEMAN-CROSS-01`, `O-LOCAL-CONT-01`  
**Status:** `PROVED_SYMBOLIC` at the operator-inequality level, with normalization constants to be pinned to the final Suzuki convention before formalization.  
**RH status:** proves a local continuation mechanism at every support radius with positive odd spectral margin; explicitly does **not** rule out a first finite zero.

## 1. Setup

Use the locally compensated support extension from `ODD_LOCAL_MEAN_COMPENSATION.md`.

Let `x` denote the old-core derivative coordinate and `n` the newly added zero-mean boundary coordinate. Let `X,H` be primitives so that, up to the project's harmless `i` convention,

`X` is the old odd `H_0^1` test function and `H` is the localized odd boundary correction.

The cross form splits into:

1. the universal logarithmic singularity;
2. the smooth archimedean remainder;
3. finitely many prime-power hinges.

The prime part is treated in `ODD_PRIME_CROSS_LOCALIZATION.md`.

## 2. Universal adjacent-boundary singularity

Suzuki's local screw expansion has principal term

`g_0(t)=(1/2)|t| log|t| + A|t|`.

After one integration by parts in each derivative variable, the cross interaction of the `|t|log|t|` part between the old side and an adjacent new boundary side is represented, away from the diagonal, by the Carleman kernel

`1/(s+t)`, `s,t>0`,

up to the fixed coefficient coming from `(1/2)|t|log|t|`.

The linear `A|t|` contribution is finite rank / moment-type after the same integrations and is absorbed into the smooth/finite-dimensional bookkeeping.

## 3. Carleman inequality

The classical Hilbert integral inequality gives

`| integral_0^infty integral_0^infty F(s) G(t)/(s+t) ds dt |`
`<= pi ||F||_2 ||G||_2`.

Therefore the universal singular old/new cross term satisfies

`|C_sing(x,n)| <= C_Car ||X||_2 ||H||_2`,

where `C_Car` is the explicit normalization constant (at most `pi/2` per oriented principal `(1/2)|t|log|t|` interaction before symmetric-component bookkeeping).

The important point is **scale invariance**: the singular cross operator does not become small merely because the new boundary width goes to zero.

This is why an absolute `L2` support-step argument cannot prove global positivity by itself.

## 4. Smooth archimedean remainder

For the `C^2` local remainder and for cross-boundary regions separated from the diagonal, two integrations by parts produce a bounded kernel.

On any fixed finite support horizon `a<=A`, its contribution has the form

`|C_smooth(x,n)| <= C_smooth(A) ||X||_2 ||H||_2`,

and on genuinely thin separated pieces one can retain additional powers of the boundary width. No such extra smallness is needed for the qualitative reduction below.

## 5. Prime-power cross term

By `O-PRIME-CROSS-01`, after choosing the boundary width below half the minimum log-spacing of the finitely many active prime powers,

`|C_prime(x,n)|`
`<= ||X||_2 ||H||_2`
`   * (sum_{q active} Lambda(q)^2/q)^(1/2)`

(up to the explicit symmetric normalization factor).

Classically the square-root factor is `O(a)` at support radius `a`.

Thus, for every fixed finite `a`, there is an explicit finite constant `C(a)` such that

`boxed: |C_{a,delta}(x,n)| <= C(a) ||X||_2 ||H||_2`.

The constant can be sharpened component-by-component; the existence of a finite primitive-norm bound is the structural point.

## 6. New-boundary coercivity

By the adaptive/local boundary results, sufficiently thin locally compensated boundary coordinates satisfy

`Q_new(n) >= L(a,delta) ||H||_2^2`

with

`L(a,delta) -> +infinity`

as `delta->0`; the leading growth is logarithmic:

`L(a,delta) = log(1/delta)+O_a(1)`.

Fresh prime thresholds contribute only the explicitly controlled `O(w_q delta^2)` shock correction and do not change this divergence.

## 7. Primitive-norm Schur loss

For `u=x+n`,

`Q_b(u)=Q_a(x)+2 Re C_{a,delta}(x,n)+Q_new(n)`.

Using the previous two bounds,

`2|C| <= epsilon L ||H||^2 + C(a)^2/(epsilon L) ||X||^2`.

Optimizing/completing the square gives the sufficient lower bound

`Q_b(x+n)`
`>= Q_a(x) - [C(a)^2/L(a,delta)] ||X||_2^2`.

This is the exact reason a sufficiently thin boundary extension preserves positivity at every point where the old odd form has a genuine positive lowest eigenvalue.

## 8. Local continuation theorem

Suppose at support radius `a`

`Q_a(x) >= lambda_a ||X||_2^2`

for all old odd test vectors, with `lambda_a>0`.

Choose `delta` so that

`L(a,delta) > C(a)^2/lambda_a`.

Then

`Q_{a+delta}(u)>0`

for every nonzero enlarged odd vector (with the equality/null-direction details handled using strict new-block positivity).

### Theorem O-LOCAL-CONT-01

Strict odd Weil positivity is locally open in support radius, with an explicit support-step certificate generated by the local boundary decomposition, Carleman cross bound, and prime-strip localization.

This is compatible with Suzuki's independent continuity theorem for the lowest localized eigenvalue. It is not a stronger global result.

## 9. Why this does not prove RH

The required step size depends on the current positive margin `lambda_a`.

If RH were false, or if a support-propagation route encountered its first degeneracy, then

`lambda_a -> 0`

as the critical support is approached. The permissible adaptive steps may then shrink fast enough to accumulate at a finite radius (a Zeno obstruction).

Therefore local continuation, even with arbitrarily small steps and a positive new diagonal block, cannot cross a hypothetical first null radius.

This is not a technical nuisance. It isolates the exact load-bearing statement:

`boxed: no nonzero odd null vector exists at any finite support radius.`

Equivalently in derivative variables:

`ker(G_a | E_a^0) = {0}`

for every finite `a`.

By Suzuki/Yoshida this nondegeneracy statement is RH-strength.

### Lane H — rank-one/Herglotz reductions
