\boxed{\mathrm{RH}\iff\Psi(t)\ge0\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb R.}
\]

Here \(\Phi(z,s,a)\) in \(B\) is the Hurwitz–Lerch transcendent, not the Riemann–Pólya kernel used in §§4–9.

The prime sum reveals a useful mechanical picture. Distributionally,

\[
\Psi''(t)
=A(t)-\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,
\delta(t-\log n),
\]

with

\[
A(t)=e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2}
-\frac{e^{-t/2}}{1-e^{-2t}}
=\frac{e^{3t}-e^t-1}{e^{t/2}(e^{2t}-1)}.
\]

Thus the smooth background curvature changes sign exactly once, at

\[
t_c=\log\rho\approx0.2811995743,
\qquad \rho^3=\rho+1.
\]

Because \(t_c<\log2\), every interval after the first prime-power event is strictly convex, while each prime power produces a downward jump in slope.

This can be compressed into a sequence of exact scalar barriers. List the prime powers as \(q_1<q_2<\cdots\), set

\[
b_j=\log q_j,\qquad
w_j=\frac{\Lambda(q_j)}{\sqrt{q_j}},\qquad
W_k=\sum_{j\le k}w_j,\qquad
D_k=\sum_{j\le k}w_jb_j.
\]

On \(b_k\le t\le b_{k+1}\),

\[
\Psi(t)=B(t)-tW_k+D_k.
\]

Consequently RH is equivalent to the infinite family

\[
\boxed{
M_k:=D_k-\max_{b_k\le t\le b_{k+1}}
\{tW_k-B(t)\}\ge0
\quad(k\ge1),
}
\]

together with the initial interval \(0\le t\le\log2\), whose positivity Suzuki proves unconditionally. Since \(B''>0\) on every later interval, the maximizer is the unique solution of \(B'(t)=W_k\) when that solution lies inside the interval, and otherwise an endpoint. This is a genuine reduction in local complexity—one convex minimization per prime-power prefix—but not a proof: the accumulated downward shocks still require a uniform global bound.

A numerical reconnaissance through \(q_k\le10^7\) (665,134 prime-power events) found the smallest computed barrier

\[
M_k\approx0.0275205733534
\]

on the interval \((3089,3109)\), with the active point \(e^t\approx3099.70976\). This is finite-range evidence only. It cannot be promoted to a uniform margin: Suzuki notes that RH together with simplicity and linear independence of the ordinates would imply \(\liminf_{t\to\infty}\Psi(t)=0\).

#### 10.6.1 Cycle-II Fenchel-energy refinement

The prefix barriers admit a useful global Fenchel form. Since \(B''(t)>0\) and is increasing for \(t>t_c\), define

\[
\tau(w)=(B')^{-1}(w),
\qquad
B^*(w)=w\tau(w)-B(\tau(w)),
\qquad
E_k=D_k-B^*(W_k).
\]

Then

\[
\min_{t>t_c}\{B(t)-tW_k+D_k\}=E_k,
\qquad M_k\ge E_k.
\]

Although \(E_k\ge0\) is stronger than the corresponding single local condition \(M_k\ge0\), the **full family is equivalent to RH**. Indeed, set

\[
P_k(t)=B(t)-tW_k+D_k.
\]

For every real \(t\),

\[
P_k(t)-\Psi(t)
=\sum_{j\le k}w_j(b_j-t)_+
+\sum_{j>k}w_j(t-b_j)_+
\ge0.
\]

Thus \(\Psi\ge0\) implies every \(P_k\ge0\), hence every \(E_k\ge0\). Conversely, on \(b_k\le t\le b_{k+1}\), one has \(P_k(t)=\Psi(t)\). If all \(E_k\ge0\), these intervals give \(\Psi(t)\ge0\) for \(t\ge\log2\), while Suzuki proves the initial interval unconditionally. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RH}\iff E_k\ge0\quad\text{for every }k.
}
\]

The exact one-step law is

\[
E_k-E_{k-1}
=w_kb_k-\int_{W_{k-1}}^{W_k}\tau(u)\,du.
\]

Writing \(\tau_k=\tau(W_k)\), this is also

\[
E_k-E_{k-1}
=w_k(b_k-\tau_k)+D_{B^*}(W_{k-1},W_k)
\ge
w_k(b_k-\tau_k)+\frac{w_k^2}{2B''(\tau_k)}.
\]

If \(b(u)=b_k\) on \(W_{k-1}<u\le W_k\), the accumulated form is

\[
E_k=E_0+\int_0^{W_k}[b(u)-\tau(u)]\,du.
\]

This reframes the desired bound as integrated dominance between the prime-power shock locations and the quantiles generated by the archimedean curvature. Pointwise dominance is false. A second exact form shows why ordinary prime-number-theorem bounds do not close it.

At a prefix endpoint \(x=q_k\), write

\[
W(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
F(x)=B'(\log x),
\qquad
H(x)=W(x)-F(x).
\]

With

\[
\kappa=-\frac\pi4-\frac12\{\gamma+\log(8\pi)\},
\]

the archimedean derivative has the exact expansion

\[
F(x)=2\sqrt x+\kappa
+2\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{x^{-(4m+1)/2}}{4m+1}.
\]

Define the endpoint Bregman penalty

\[
Q(x)=B^*(W(x))-B^*(F(x))-\log x\,[W(x)-F(x)]\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
E(x)=\Psi(\log x)-Q(x)
=-\int_1^x\frac{H(u)}u\,du-Q(x).
}
\]

Thus the target is not merely an average prime-counting estimate: the signed accumulated discrepancy must also dominate its nonnegative endpoint energy.

For \(R(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)-x\), put

\[
J_R(x)=\int_1^x\frac{R(u)}{u^{3/2}}
\left(1+\frac12\log\frac xu\right)du.
\]

If \(G\) denotes Catalan’s constant, set

\[
\beta=\frac{\pi^2+8G}{4}-8,
\quad
c_0=4+\beta=0.2993322886\ldots,
\quad
a_0=-(1+\kappa)=1.6860917096\ldots,
\]

and

\[
r_B(x)=-4\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{x^{-(4m+1)/2}}{(4m+1)^2}.
\]

Partial summation gives the exact endpoint identity

\[
\boxed{
E(x)=c_0-a_0\log x+r_B(x)-J_R(x)-Q(x).
}
\]

All leading PNT asymptotics cancel. Current explicit zero-free-region bounds give only an unsigned envelope for \(J_R\) of order

\[
\sqrt x(\log x)^{1.515}e^{-0.8274\sqrt{\log x}},
\]

far larger than the required logarithmic scale. Even a hypothetical RH-size pointwise estimate \(R(x)=O(\sqrt x\log^2x)\) gives only an unsigned \(O(\log^4x)\) bound. The missing information is signed cancellation/correlation, not merely a sharper absolute PNT error.

A long-double reconnaissance through \(x\le10^9\), covering 50,851,223 prime-power events, found no negative margin. Its smallest value was

\[
E(x)\approx0.0214985808236747
\qquad\text{at }x=34{,}186{,}367.
\]

This scan is not interval-certified and proves nothing beyond its finite range. The Fenchel family is an exact RH-equivalent compression and a clean proof target, not an RH proof.

#### 10.6.2 Reproduction note: a minor printed constant in Suzuki

Differentiating the displayed \(B(t)\) gives, on \(0<t<\log2\),

\[
B'(t)=2(e^{t/2}-e^{-t/2})+c
-\arctan(e^{t/2})+\operatorname{arctanh}(e^{-t/2}),
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
c=\frac\pi4-\frac12\{\gamma+\log(8\pi)\}.
}
\]

Suzuki’s journal paper and arXiv v4 print \(c=\pi/4-(\gamma+3\log2)/2\), omitting \(-\tfrac12\log\pi\). The correction follows directly from the linear term \(\tfrac t2[\psi(1/4)-\log\pi]\), the identity \(\psi(1/4)=-\gamma-\pi/2-3\log2\), and differentiation of the Lerch term. Two model instances reconstructed the calculation separately. The corrected constant reproduces the paper’s reported critical points

\[
0.1526309144549\ldots,
\qquad
0.4640017896088\ldots,
\]

whereas the printed constant produces no such roots on \((0,\log2)\). This is therefore a typographical omission in an intermediate displayed formula, not a flaw in Theorem 4.1: the numerical work evidently used the correct constant, and \(B''\), convexity, and the screw-function criterion are unaffected. Any reproduction of \(B'\), \(\tau=(B')^{-1}\), or the Fenchel margins must use the corrected value. No published erratum or corrected arXiv version was located as of 2026-08-01; the proportionate action is a private note to the author or journal, not a research-priority claim.

The shifted functions \(\Psi_\omega\) do not propagate positivity backward toward the critical line. If \(\mathcal T_\omega\) denotes Suzuki’s transform in equation (11.1), direct differentiation gives

\[
(\mathcal T_\omega f)''(t)=e^{-\omega t}f''(t).
\]

But \(\mathcal T_\omega\) is not order-reflecting: on \(t\ge0\),

\[
f(t)=t-\frac{t^2}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal T_1f(t)=1-e^{-t}>0,
\]

although \(f(t)<0\) for \(t>2\). Positivity in a known zero-free shifted half-plane therefore cannot simply be inverted to prove \(\Psi\ge0\).

Suzuki’s 2026 framework separately gives a continuous-function realization of Weil’s quadratic form and formulates limiting self-adjoint operators without assuming RH. These are rigorous reformulations, but the global cross-prime cancellation or operator positivity remains the conjectural step; this is not yet a completed Hilbert–Pólya construction.

## 11. Review-process integrity and metacognitive reflection

### 11.1 Review questions and search protocol

Cycle II separated four questions that are often collapsed:

1. Is the mathematical statement correct?
2. Is the statement already in the literature?
3. If not, is the strengthening useful rather than merely different?
4. What level of external validation is required before public circulation?

The literature work was a **rapid scoping review**, not a PRISMA-compliant systematic review and not a MathSciNet review. Searches were run on 2026-08-01 across arXiv, publisher or journal full text, institutional repositories, author pages, EuDML/Numdam, and exact-phrase web indexes. Secondary aggregators were used only to discover candidate sources; the decisive claims were checked in primary full text.

The principal query families combined:

- “generalized/extended Laguerre inequalities” with “finite,” “for every \(N\),” and “counterexample”;
- “admissible kernel” with “nonreal zeros,” “positive definite,” and “Laguerre–Pólya”;
- “Fourier transform,” “strictly decreasing,” “super-Gaussian,” and “zero strip”;
- “Epstein zeta,” “Eisenstein series,” “regularized Mellin transform,” and products of completed zeta functions;
- official arXiv, AMS, COPE, and CRediT policy pages for reporting and attribution.

Inclusion required an exact theorem, construction, formula, or policy bearing on a promoted claim. Sources that merely mentioned RH or Laguerre inequalities were excluded. Citation chains were followed around Muranaka, Csordas, Newman, Cardon, and the Eisenstein/Epstein literature. The search did not have subscription access to MathSciNet or a complete zbMATH similarity search; that is a material limitation on any assertion that something was *not* previously known.

### 11.2 Explicit multi-agent architecture

| Track | Initial information policy | Required output | Cross-check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior-art/novelty | Searched independently from the proof track | Exact closest theorem, source, and publication-safe novelty wording | Root retrieved decisive full texts; referee challenged overclaims |
| Proof continuation | Asked for a new mechanism, not status | Concrete identity, lemma, construction, or counterexample | Constants and domains checked by referee and root |
| Adversarial referee/reporting | Given promoted claims and known failure modes | Correctness verdict, closest precedent, reporting threshold, red flags | Compared against primary sources and explicit calculations |
| Root synthesis | Maintained the claim registry and proof standard | Integrated verdict and durable dossier | Reconciled disagreements and downgraded claims |

These were **multi-instance LLM consistency checks**, not independent peer review. The tracks shared model lineage, training data, and likely blind spots. Agreement among them raises confidence less than agreement among genuinely independent mathematicians. This is why the reporting protocol requires external human reconstruction.
